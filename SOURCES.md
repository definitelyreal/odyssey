What was found, what was verified, what was substituted, what could not be sourced.

## A1 — Recited Homeric Greek with lyre
- **Bundled:** `assets/a1-danek-hagel.mp3` — Georg Danek (Univ. Vienna) & Stefan Hagel (Austrian
  Academy of Sciences), Odyssey viii 266–366 (Demodokos sings of Ares and Aphrodite), sung in
  reconstructed pronunciation to a reconstructed four-string phorminx. Trimmed to 3:02 with a
  fade for the jukebox format (full recording is 5:03).
- Rights: the Danek–Hagel academic reconstruction, freely circulated for scholarly and educational
  listening (featured by Open Culture, BoingBoing, Greek City Times); not formally public domain.
  Credited on the card. Their authoritative host (oeaw.ac.at) blocks automated fetches, so the
  audio was pulled from the circulating SoundCloud copy (soundcloud.com/user444756202/demodokos).
- Substitution note: the brief imagined the poem's opening; the canonical Danek–Hagel recording is
  the Demodokos passage from Book viii, so the on-screen Greek is viii 266–273 (Greek Wikisource,
  verified against the audio's passage) — the sung text, not the proem. The proem's first line
  appears in B1 instead.
- Link out on the card: Open Culture feature (verified 200).

## A2 — Avdo Međedović, 1935
- **Bundled:** `assets/a2-mededovic.mp3` (1:41) — soundtrack of "Avdo Međedović, Guslar," the
  Center for Hellenic Studies (Harvard) video built on Milman Parry's 1935 aluminum-disc field
  recording from Bijelo Polje. License: Creative Commons Attribution (per the CHS upload's
  license metadata). Attribution: The Center for Hellenic Studies / Milman Parry Collection of
  Oral Literature, Harvard University.
- Embeddable backup (in records.json): YouTube `UhBq_DOEgbM` (oembed verified 200).
- Not used: Harvard CURIOSity catalog pages for the full Parry collection — bot-walled during
  verification (403/202), so not linked from the site.

## B1 — Six translators
- Chapman 1616 and Pope 1725: full invocations, Project Gutenberg (ebooks 48895 and 3160),
  extracted verbatim from the raw HTML and shown in full (public domain). Links verified 200.
- Lattimore 1965 (HarperCollins sample PDF + cross-checks), Fitzgerald 1961 (Purdue comparison
  page + cross-checks), Fagles 1996 (Bryn Mawr Classical Review), Wilson 2017 (verified quote
  page + cross-checks): first line or two ONLY, with link-outs to HarperCollins, Macmillan/FSG,
  Penguin Random House, and W. W. Norton respectively. All four in copyright — no extended
  passages reproduced.
- Greek first line from Perseus (Hom. Od. 1.1), verified.
- Note: the Macmillan link-out sits behind Cloudflare bot protection (curl shows 403) but loads
  normally in a real browser — verified in a browser session.
